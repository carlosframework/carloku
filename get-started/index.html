Get started.

This is the head half; the heart half is that you'll be live before it feels like work. Four steps, one small tool.

1 · Install the CLI

brew install carlosframework/tap/carlos

Not on Homebrew? Prebuilt binaries live at github.com/carlosframework/releases. It's one static binary: put it on your PATH and you're done.

2 · Sign in

Create your account at console.carloku.com — you sign in with a passkey through Keymail, so there's no password to invent. Then connect your terminal:

carlos auth login -console https://console.carloku.com

The CLI prints a short code; you approve it in the browser you're already signed in to. That's the whole ceremony.

3 · Create and ship

carlos apps create -app hello
carlos ship -app hello -kind static -version v1 ./public

Shipping uploads exactly what's in the directory and gives your app a private address you can open right away. Version strings are yours to choose; a git short SHA is the house convention.

4 · Promote

carlos promote -app hello v1 stable

Promotion is the "go" button: the release you just looked at starts serving for real. To roll back, promote the previous version: it's the same command pointed at an older name. One guard-rail: stable is protected by your passkey, so the CLI hands you to the console to approve it — earlier channels promote straight from the terminal.

Once you're comfortable, the two steps collapse into one motion:

carlos deploy -app hello ./hello-linux-arm64

It ships, promotes, and waits until your URL serves the new build — when it prints live, it is. The session on the homepage is a real capture of exactly that, and the app it deployed is really running.

Carloku is young and moving quickly. If anything on this page surprises you, the console is the source of truth, and the platform underneath is open source.

Under the hood

For the technically curious, this is the part we keep off the front page:

  • A release is an immutable, content-addressed artifact. Promotion moves a signed pointer; nothing is ever rebuilt between preview and production, and every box verifies the signature before running a release.
  • Channels carry releases through canary and bake stages, so production only adopts what has already served traffic.
  • Idle apps hibernate: the process stops, the route stays, and the first request wakes it.
  • Custom domains get per-host certificates minted and renewed automatically.

The whole design is public — read it at carlosframework.com.
